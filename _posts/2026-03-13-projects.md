A lot of thoughts, thinking, almost often. I should feel over the top about my midterm, but I don’t as much as I’d thought. I have a class starting in a week where we make something every day for 100 days, and I’ve completely pivoted from what I’d planned earlier.

I worked on this project for my midterm. The project’s premise was really personal, and so I abstracted it a whole lot because I was scared to tap into my story. I was initially scared of presenting it during user testing and played chicken because, while presenting the storyboard, people made comments about the experience, such as “You were just a kid.” Don’t get me wrong, the concern, the care, is fair and good, but it feels uncomfortable to me, especially in the context of presenting it. However, when I started getting critiqued on the project, it felt much more normalized, so much so that the critique took away something from the trauma, in a good way. I got a lot of compliments for the project, and I felt proud of myself for presenting this.

The professor from the class told me on 5th March that I’m finding my artistic voice and growing emotionally really quickly. I kept going up to her with revelations every week. She was really proud of me, and that just meant a lot to me. My story fell behind in some ways, we both agreed. I wasn’t able to tell it the way I would have, but it also leaves people with the exact feelings I wanted them to feel, in a way I currently like. Personally, I think the project didn’t quite land, but I was able to tap into something personal. Even though this was really a small part of the story, I know I can work on larger parts without being scared now.

Every time you make personal artwork, I realise it shapes who you are as a person, and I suppose that is, in some ways, finding your artistic voice, and that trauma left me with immense anxiety, intertwining with my PTSD, trickling down onto every aspect of my life, every time that I don’t have control. But I can actively feel myself letting go, trying to have control, and I can feel myself getting less anxious.

But working on this made me realise that while I do love making films, animating, and illustrating, it might not be my medium to tell stories. It doesn’t intersect all of my interests. I can’t see myself just making films. I like very specific aspects of this medium, but I can’t bank on just doing those. But, I’ll still keep telling stories through films, but perhaps it won’t be something that I focus on. I’m still figuring it all out. This is what I feel in the moment.

On the other hand, I really enjoyed making a radio for my roommate. She was recalling her favorite memory with her dad: listening to the radio in the car. So I made her a hand-crank radio. Making this project made me realise that perhaps I want to tell stories in a more tangible manner. I want to create physical objects that tell stories. It challenges me in a manner that I like.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/love-bug.jpg)

Another project I started really late but really enjoyed is making two bug bots. I called them ‘love bugs’. My professor told me to add personality to one of the bug bots, so I made it have a random mood setting that was dependent on the number of button presses, and it would then move toward or away from the other one. However, I didn’t have enough time for the project, so I just got the bug bot to flap its wings the same way.

But this project also made me realise how far behind I am in understanding circuits and in fabrication skills.

So I thought I could use 100 days of making to learn the basics of BEAM robotics. The final outcome of the class would be to make two bugs, maybe two beetles or two ants, to show things that I love about my best friend and aspects of our friendship that have made it so long-lasting.

- That they reciprocate love (moving toward the other bot)
    
- That they are there for me (if one robot finds something, it signals the other robot)
    
- That we quietly understand each other (avoid obstacles)
    
- That we change (changes in direction)
    
- That we grow (movement in speed increases)
    
- That we are sometimes together and sometimes separate (two separate bots that can connect together to form a walker)
    
- That we observe each other (a bot that can detect things)
    

I’ve also realised something. I can stay really well in this realm of making things for people that are rather sentimental, and tell stories too. But I want to make heavier artwork. Someone commented after watching my film in class that, in some ways, it did attempt what this quote says: “Art should comfort the disturbed and disturb the comfortable,” by Cesar A. Cruz. So, I was able to make an abstracted film that partially tells my story, but can I make physical things that are heavier, that tell my story too, and perhaps that’s the challenge I want to take on?

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/me.jpg)

I’m finding some form of solace in solitude more than I thought. These days, I run out of social battery much more quickly than before. Much of it is spent on the floor in a day. Over the last weekend, I realized that in many of my painful relationships, I left quietly, long before they ended. My partners failed to understand that quietness, which made me aware of how I feel and observe a lot quietly. While I can express myself as I write, the words I speak fail to truly reflect how I feel. While I might come across as someone who talks a lot, about different aspects of my life, I’m actually pretty quiet. Sometimes, this quietness gets lost in misunderstanding and miscommunication. However, when I sense that someone understands me quietly, I feel close to them. This made me realize the importance of a quiet kind of understanding.
