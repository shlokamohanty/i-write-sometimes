Four years of my life before moving to New York now feel like a distant past, even though it’s nearer than I’d imagine. I was so detached during the four years that even my memories feel foggy. Coming out of school with unprocessed trauma, and going into undergrad right after, with so much change and so much newness, in the first year, I romanticised everything, and quickly, right after, got into this state of numbness. Perhaps, because of this very feeling, I couldn’t form deeper relationships, as I refused to share what was happening in my life with others.

Since moving to New York, things have been different. I share a lot more about what’s going on in my life with people, and I’m processing all of the unprocessed trauma, memories, and feelings that come in ebbs and flows. But I feel an innate sense of gratefulness for the people around me. So, I’ve decided that this semester, I will dedicate a project to each of the people that I feel most grateful for.

But Wisconsin is still dear to me. I met a lot of people, although I wasn’t able to share a whole lot about myself with them, it still felt sweet in parts. These are some places and people I miss the most today.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image_wisconsin_pines1.jpg)

Leopold’s was my favorite cafe in Madison. The barista knew my friends and I. They’d give us free samples to try out. They had the best tiramisu, or that’s what I thought then. My friends and I spent a lot of time here, and whenever anyone would visit us, the first thing we’d do was bring them here. Perhaps, at some points, we were really noisy. I’d often carry my camera to Leopold’s, and it was so fun to capture all the moments with my friends then. Except once on my way back from Leopold’s, I climbed a tree and dropped my camera, but the camera stayed intact. The camera has been with me since 2012. The cafe meant a lot to me, then.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image_wisconsin_pines2.jpg)

The sunsets on the docks in Madison, to this date, can’t be compared. I remember my first winter in Madison, it was rough, but when spring came along, the clocks turned forward, days became longer, and everyone was out on the docks. We’d spend time just soaking in the sun. We’d frequent about watching the sunset, and it was beautiful. In summer, we even swam in Lake Mendota.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image_wisconsin_pines3.jpg)

I love fireflies, and Picnic Point in Madison was one of the first places where I saw them during the summer. Picnic Point was a nature trail. I have walked there and back so often. I’ve seen it when the lake was frozen in winter and in summer, and it is beautiful seeing snow on the beach and waves frozen in time. I’ve had some really memorable moments here throughout the time that I was there.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image_wisconsin_pines4.jpg)

Bascom Hill, to me, was like how the Williamsburg Bridge is right now. When I feel really low in New York, I walk across the Williamsburg Bridge. I’ve even cried on the bridge. The cold wind hitting my face helps. Bascom Hill was something like that for me. I’d go walk the hill when I felt low. But Bascom Hill was also one of the places where I just spent time hanging out with friends, having conversations, even studying and doing parts of my chemistry lab. The hill was something special. Walking up and down the hill was always tedious, though, especially during winter.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image_wisconsin_pines5.jpg)

Megan was my undergrad roommate for three years, though I was away from the apartment for a period during that time. While we lived together, she would often tell me I wasn’t vulnerable enough, encouraging me to share more, but I usually shied away and hid in my room. Recently, she mentioned that she’s gotten to know me much better now than during our four years at undergrad. I miss her a lot. During our time as roommates, at some points in the three years, we became homebodies. She’d still say I’m a homebody now, and I remember how we’d often wait on each other before going out. Across my three summers in Madison, I spent two of them with her. We’d drink tea, and I loved asking about her day because she’d recount it with the smallest details, telling stories in a spiral. She’s probably annoyed with me right now, since I haven’t called in weeks and even forgot to call her over the weekend.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image_wisconsin_pines6.jpg)

Tejaswi, my best friend from undergrad. I sit like this, too, and I miss him a whole lot, too. Freshman year of undergrad, during the second semester, we ended up studying together almost every other day, then I became his roommate temporarily, and crashed on his couch. When we were in Calculus 3 together and decided to pull an all-nighter to study, we took breaks and watched ‘Rick and Morty’ in increments, but we definitely didn’t study productively as we should have. With him, I did get vulnerable. He threw up on me once, very early on, when we had initially become friends. Well, we were in high school together, but he came in during the 11th grade. Then COVID happened, so we never really spoke, but we became really close in undergrad. We both understood each other well, still do, without much explanation as such. I miss Kronshage Residence Hall and spending time with him.
