Thinking about using processes I learnt over undergrad, as a framework for how to approach projects next semester. Going about how I worked undergrad, maybe a good step to move forward with, since I didn't come from an arts background, and it may be more helpful to create a methodology based on my STEM experiences.

One of the things that immediately comes to mind from that is the pre-lab, lab, and post-lab methodology. From my chemistry labs, as far as I can remember, we had to write all the materials and step-by-step instructions on the process of the lab. The lab involved the actual data collection, and the post-lab involved analysis.

In some ways, this would be me, building a framework around metabolizing experiences rather than suppressing it or dumping it raw, like in my previous works. Metabolizing experiences is a slower process. ITP doesn't give you space to do that. I'm trying to build a slower practice in such a fast-paced environment. I wonder, this would thus would this mean I should rely on experiences in the past, rather than ones that are happening in the moment with immediacy? But working with past things is more archaeological. Perhaps, some form of deliberate lag, of a few months, maybe.

This is the framework I’m using for ‘Rewiring Connections’. Perhaps, I can use it for most of my solo projects in the next semester.

I think I want to start approaching projects with a question that I want to answer. So a hypothesis essentially.

I feel like art and science, and art and technology are different. There's an intersection for all three, but the inquiries are different. That's a confusion that I have with ITP as well. There are a few courses that touch upon arts and sciences as an intersection, but it is mostly art and technology. Maybe I am in the wrong place, or I'm not trying enough, or in the right manner.

On not trying enough: Urmika and Megan told me that in undergrad, I used to be super busy, and I tried to make time for people and did fun things. Apoorv would complain that I didn't make enough time for him. So, all my time went into studying and relationships. I didn't make any time for myself. Urmika and Megan were shocked at how I was managing time then. Maybe that's why, coming into ITP, I'm used to the workload. But I'm still not sectioning it well, so building a framework is a must for next semester. Ugh, Shloka in 10th grade knew that the Media Lab was the place. Now, I'm not brave enough to even dream of that. Nor am I qualified enough. Maybe someday.

I think there's a privilege to being young, in comparison to my peers. In some ways, being young means there are fewer consequences when making mistakes. By that, I don't mean grave errors, but smaller things, like being confused about career paths or certain choices. My peers have a lot of other commitments, and having fewer anchors in that sense is helpful. I have more freedom to try out different paths. There's a lot of naivety here as I'm writing this. I wanted to try death literature, so I started reading "Tuesdays with Morrie", but it's so cheesy. I don't like it. But since I started, I have to finish. But this quote did stick out: "If you accept you can die anytime, maybe you're not ambitious enough". Revolving around my anxieties with death and allergies, which partly in undergrad, my brain was more focused on that. So, more energy and time were spent on that. There was some acceptance, during that time, of a mediocre life, feeling entirely detached while studying economics, that I'm going to end up in a boring job, and this is it. But that junior year summer is when it all changed, and I finally accepted that this is not what I want to do in life. So, I'm on the more hopeful end now. Baryon asymmetry, and how the universe was rigged in favor of existence. With perfect symmetry, the universe would have lent itself to nothingness. Imbalance. Complexity. I remember when I first moved to New York, I liked the fact that it was crowded, so it feels like I can disappear easily. Which is a painful thing to rather feel, sort of ambitiousless. It's different now. I like that it's crowded, in a manner in which I can observe and learn more. It's stimulating in a manner that. Thus, I can dream bigger, naively — tenured professor instead of an adjunct professor, haha.
