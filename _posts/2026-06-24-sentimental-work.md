<span class="letter-to">Sentimentality</span>
 <div class="letter-block">24th June 2026,
Spitballing. I personally don't think making tragic/angsty angry work is something that's in me. It doesn't turn out well, because I don't actually wallow in self-pity all that much, and I don't necessarily dwell on tragedies, nor do I feel angry. Things happen, and you get over it. I don't believe in the 'good damage' trope of it all. That's why the 'Shadows by the blue light' wasn't perhaps my best storytelling work, because it focused too much on the tragic part. I do like work that's raw, unapologetically honest, perhaps, like my 'Rewiring Connections' project, which does carry a lot of nuance to it. It's a little wry as well. But, I think, I do like making work that's about love. I was telling Kezia about art and its capability to manipulate, and she explained to me that it's only one kind of work. The more emotional ones. She employs humor in more of her work. In terms of my work, I think it reflects my deep internal desires to be loved, perhaps, even though I actively avoid it in reality. I don't think it's in opposition to one another. The avoidance and the desire are at play with one another. So maybe, time to time, it's okay for me to make sentimental work as such, I can lean on the raw, unapologetically honest work, when I have the breadth, the time, the vulnerability, and when I don't, I can lean more into sentimental work, in regards to romantic love, I guess, and that's alright.

Or maybe, there's more depth to it. Most of it comes out in a raw form, rather than something that is processed. The manner in which I treat some of my other projects. With the Decomposition -> Reconstruction -> Interpretation. Maybe, because I let it out in the most raw form, it turns out to be cute and cheesy. Or maybe that desire spilling out in the form of being 'cute' and 'cheesy' is the avoidance. Or maybe, there's no hidden architecture to any of my more sentimental work at all.
</div>

 <div class="letter-block">16th July 2026,
I stand by my statement that sentimentality is inherently manipulative. I think I just needed to understand what it means, exactly. It’s not really okay for me to make sentimental art, even from time to time. I need to be more responsible. I think what I meant then is that, to me, when I was referring to it earlier, I was thinking about it in a manner that all emotional art is inherently sentimental. Drives people to feel in a specific manner. I think that’s not true, though. I don’t think all emotional artwork is sentimental. Sentimentality simplifies artwork. It’s self-indulgent. That’s the manner of a lot of my artwork till now. I still haven’t made something yet that completely speaks of who I am, and how I am really capable of understanding complexity. I guess the sentimental artwork is sort of a form of having fun, for me, escapism from reality.

I think, as a person, because of the things I’ve been through, life has been complicated, but I tend to see things from my perspective instead of being truly empathetic, trying to understand other perspectives. A lot of it, I learnt along the way last year, as I confronted pretty much all of my past relationships, and even the friendships that ended. A lot of the relationships where I just left rather quietly, I did the thing to look at it more empathetically from their perspectives and even reach out. In those situations, I took the time to think about the nuances, the reasons why the people acted the way they did, and the way I did. Granted, I’m young. I’m only 23. I’ve hardly seen life, but from whatever I saw, there’s a lot that I needed to step back and look at. The smaller picture, the people. The world is simply not one or the other. Emotions are so complex, people are so complex, and I can see that- maybe I couldn’t see that as well before, but now I can. I want to make artwork that’s emotional, not sentimental. Let people confront with the more complex realities of life, challenge that perspective. 

In De Profundis by Oscar Wilde, “We think we can have our emotions for nothing,” “We cannot. Even the finest and most self-sacrificing emotions have to be paid for.” That brings me to a question: how do I know when I’m making art that I understand the cost of the emotions? I’ve made art at a comfortable distance, without trying to confront the more unflattering parts about myself. But real earnest work comes from that confrontation, which I did by looking back at my past relationships and trying to be more empathetic. I think the sentimentality comes from making art that feels familiar. Like if I make art about love, but in a cutesy way, people will feel the cutesy side of love. But I think cost comes from a place of honesty, risk, and exposure. Artwork that evokes a response that they didn’t know that I felt this way. Maybe the cost is comfort, my ego, the image that I want to present. I guess when Amisha interacted with ‘Rewiring-Connections’, they felt the complexity of the childhood we both experienced.
