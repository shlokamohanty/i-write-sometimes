I personally don't think making tragic work is something that's in me. It doesn't turn out well, because I don't actually wallow in self-pity all that much, and I don't necessarily dwell on tragedies. Things happen, and you get over it. I don't believe in the 'good damage' trope of it all. That's why the 'Shadows by the blue light' wasn't perhaps my best storytelling work, because it focused too much on the tragic part. I do like work that's raw, unapologetically honest, perhaps, like my 'Rewiring Connections' project, which does carry a lot of nuance to it. But, I think, I do like making work that's about love. I was telling Kezia about art and its capability to manipulate, and she explained to me that it's only one kind of work. The more emotional ones. She employs humor in more of her work. In terms of my work, I think it reflects my deep internal desires to be loved, perhaps, even though I actively avoid it in reality. I don't think it's in opposition to one another. The avoidance and the desire are at play with one another. So maybe, time to time, it's okay for me to make sentimental work as such, I can lean on the raw, unapologetically honest work, when I have the breadth, the time, the vulnerability, and when I don't, I can lean more into sentimental work, in regards to romantic love, I guess, and that's alright.

Or maybe, there's more depth to it. Most of it comes out in a raw form, rather than something that is processed. The manner in which I treat some of my other projects. With the Decomposition -> Reconstruction -> Interpretation. Maybe, because I let it out in the most raw form, it turns out to be cute and cheesy. Spitballing.
