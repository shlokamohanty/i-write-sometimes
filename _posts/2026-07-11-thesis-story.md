I kind of like the idea from the undergrad funny film I was making. And I think I want to start writing with that. The scientist going to different timelines to find happiness. But this is more in the context of time travel to find greater certainty?

Where in my life have I previously wanted certainty, and how has that anxiety impacted other people in my life? And what sacrifices have I made in the past or am making for certainty in the future? But, in reality, there is no certainty, so are the sacrifices even worth it? Or maybe the story of a scientist who overthinks a lot, so the time machine as a brain, is just symbolic of the person going back in time, and replaying conversations and scenarios, to find out what else they could have done, in situations, as if that would give some certainty, but in reality that still doesn't give certainty. That certainty was never actually available.

That’s the thing with memory also: every time you recall it, it quietly changes how you remember it. This could be the concept of the whole story behind this machine.

---

The synopsis of the story: 

*So this time-machine, is symbolic of an anxious brain of a scientist who overthinks everything. The machine lets them go back in time (replaying memories, as a way of going back in time) to find certainty so they stop worrying. But every time you recall a memory, or in this case go back in time, the memory alters. So the more one overthinks to get certainty, the less reliable the thing that they are certain about is. Essentially, certainty was actually never available, and not because the person isn't trying hard enough to find certainty, but essentially trying to get certainty is what is destroying and altering the consequences of anxiety.*
