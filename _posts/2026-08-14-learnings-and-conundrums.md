Kezia taught me that expressive art is not the only form of art. She taught me the importance of having conversations with people that are not about the floor and our work. Kezia's interest in animals, dogs especially, and our conversations circling around that pique my interest in semantic differences and the notion of understanding. Kezia taught me the purpose of a portfolio. Kezia taught me how to make rings from wires. Kezia taught me how to switch to different chords on the guitar by using the same finger structure. Kezia taught me to be sillier. Kezia taught me that through conversations, sometimes, you understand more about yourself. Kezia taught me about E-ink displays.

Cody taught me how to take more charge. And that in arts, and on a low budget, you have to push for things, especially for projects. I wanted to apologise desperately after we tattled on ourselves, but he made me question my need to apologise, and I realised from there that it stems from my need for clean resolutions. He did say sometimes awkwardness is actually quite powerful. He also did say I’m sentimental, that I should lean into it. But I took it as a personal offence. I’m sure he didn’t mean it offensively, of course, because I think I understand complexity, and I don’t particularly resonate with sentimentality like that. I think I was just going through something last semester. Mostly, because currently I’m toying with the Madison vs. New York Shloka conundrum. Everyone in New York doesn’t realise the extent of apathy of Madison Shloka. Cody taught me a contrary idea to ‘healing’. Cody taught me the difference between solid-core wires and stranded wires. Cody taught me that I can remove the ground and power rails in a breadboard. Cody taught me to question working with other people’s stories vs your own stories. Cody taught me to walk more confidently with my laptop on the side, instead of in front of me. Cody taught me Obsidian. Cody taught me how to find the ground wire from any cable. Cody taught me how to take a vape apart.

arjun taught me to ask for help while working. Sometimes, I get into zones where I spend a lot of time figuring out a problem myself without asking for any help. arjun taught me to be more appreciative of the courage that it takes to perform on stage. I still struggle with that sometimes. arjun taught me to ‘take care’ of myself because he says ‘take care’ a lot, and that sticks. It consciously reminds me to drink more water. arjun taught me patience, to block out longer time for things. He also taught me and made me appreciate the meaning of ‘making’. I made a movie that had a personal story attached to it. He said if I’ve processed it, I should be able to look at it from a distance. I am stubborn sometimes, and I wasn’t listening properly to what he was saying. I was in this mindset that I have to make this, and I don’t think I had processed it then. I actually can’t watch the movie now. I feel like barfing watching it now. I made it in a manner where it brought up the feeling of reliving it, and, it fell short of the story. Recently, I was making my framework. It does take into account what arjun said then, and the importance of distance in that kind of work. arjun taught me that when I'm sure of certain connections in a circuit, to move on from using jumper wires to solid-core wires. arjun taught me to use header pins while soldering an Arduino to a protoboard. arjun taught me about coin batteries. arjun also taught me to push myself as much as I can while learning. arjun taught me how to be more vulnerable. arjun taught me about copper wick. arjun taught me GitHub Desktop. arjun taught me to make mind maps.

arjun taught me how to scope. But it's a conundrum because he also taught me that just because someone says it’s a thesis idea, it doesn’t mean that I should just accept it and pursue something easier.

Cody and arjun taught me how to learn to sit with not having immediate answers and solutions. Learning to sit with not knowing. And that sparked my interest in uncertainty. They also taught me to draw out my ideas more without the fear of judgement. They also taught me how to use Illustrator.

Nikolai and Ryan taught me the importance of building good relationships.

Ryan taught me that it’s important to be outside of the floor. Ryan taught me the importance of ‘how are you doing?’

Ryan and Matt taught me to be more comfortable with crying.

arjun and Matt taught me things about typography and different typefaces, which reminded me of my fascination with ‘Geronimo Stilton’ as a kid. I’ve incorporated that style in ‘Baryon Assymmetry’ and a book that I made with Gabe.

Another conundrum that comes up for me is that I look up to arjun and Cody and their process of work, but both of them employ code and circuitry so differently. Sometimes with arjun’s work, the way the circuit looks, the variables in the line of code itself evoke something, whereas for Cody, circuitry and code are tools for a larger thing. Those are hidden. But then again, Cody’s expertise is fabrication, and it’s the thing that I don’t understand that well, currently. But I guess fabrication as a medium automatically implies a more explicit exposure. It isn’t something on the backend. So maybe in that case, they both work similarly, just have different mediums? Maybe, I guess, I'm just exploring different mediums right now.

One of the biggest things I’ve questioned is the artist label. Cody and Kezia seem to question it for themselves too. arjun seems to be more comfortable with it. But I question it too. How do you know when you can take up the responsibility? I don’t know how he takes it. I question it, especially because a lot of my things are so expressive in nature.
