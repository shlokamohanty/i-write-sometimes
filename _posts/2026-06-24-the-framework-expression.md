This framework is for expressive work.

I think drawing, writing, and filming are mediums with direct exposure and need to be handled with care. While I do make for myself, sometimes my work has an audience. This framework will help me learn which work is just for myself and which one is intended for an audience. The exposure and expressiveness of the medium can unintentionally cause harm. There’s a responsibility that I need to take there; hence, the framework.

Many people sometimes make things and then realise what it is about- Gabe and Prisha have done that. I know why I had the urge to make ‘Shadows by the blue light’: because at that point, I was under the impression that I was broken, that I could not accept love in a genuine manner without spiralling into anxiety. That if I don’t get over the anxiety caused by my abusive relationship, I won't be able to harbor any other relationships safely. After making the first version, I learnt that it felt ethereal to watch. It made me realise there is nuance. It was also because my three-year relationship that ended was both bad and good. It was very strange; I was acting extremely strange coming out of it, because it wasn’t by any means healthy. The processing and effects transpired into immense anxiety. **Sometimes, making while you are feeling something helps you learn what it is about.**

**Without looking at the structure of the medium, it is actually very easy to fall into the work being extremely expressive.** That’s why some of my work is hard to look at after making, like ‘Shadows by the blue light’ and my ‘Rewiring Connections: the log’.

**I think there’s a difference between reliving while making and for/after processing.** I think this is what arjun meant by distance while making, but I’m not entirely sure. For ‘Shadows by the blue light’, as strange as it sounds, it felt like I had to relive it to make it, which makes looking at it now hard. I wasn’t looking at it from outside.

I think writing letters is that good middle, where I’m vulnerable; so was ‘Rewiring Connections: the project’. **But the nature of the log makes it harder to continue the project, even though it’s a living project, because it became a microblog accidentally. Even though it was ~~really~~ actually important for me to do the project to process.**

There is something that I'm doing there, which is that in my black holes essay right now, with a daily practice, I think of what I'm feeling in the present moment- this felt emotion- and reach for it from when I was a kid, when I think I felt similarly. But this one is not out of anxiety, or even if it is, it's looking at the vastness of anxiety from afar, rather than living anxiety. **The reframe is to approach projects with distance by looking at expressive work with awe.**

I’m ~~realising~~ noticing why letters become a good medium for me to write with than just journaling; **there’s a boundary, because it is directed towards someone.** I think the structure gives the distance. I think **if I’m able to see the nuance of the situation, and if I am, then I’ve processed.** Alanna was talking about this in some way, imagining an audience. Something I’ve mentioned in the [rewiring connections log](https://shlokamohanty.github.io/i-write-sometimes/2026/08/25/rewiring-connections-log.html). I do that sometimes, but not always; maybe I should do it throughout, so I don’t get callous.

**I think the other thing would be that if I'm anxious, I shouldn't make expressive art then. Anxiety clouds judgment and causes recklessness. I'm a person who thinks very slowly; making art while anxious would be unprocessed will have a tendency to cause harm.**

I think there are three ways to look at this: making as processing, making for processing, making after processing. The 'making as processing' and 'making for processing' become a means for me to ~~realise~~ notice something, which I often call the 'nuance', but I need to think about the distinguishing line for 'making for processing’ to be something for an audience. 

So questions to ask myself:
- Can I see the situation from many different lenses? Or am I trying to see through a different lens in this project?
- Where and why is this feeling that I have to do this project stemming from?
- What is the structure of the project? How should I log the process and keep track of what I write there, so I can pivot if need be, or form a better structure, so it becomes for processing rather than as processing?
- Can I revisit the project while making, again and again? 
    - Can I step away and come back? 
    - Can I keep returning?
