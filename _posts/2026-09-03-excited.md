6th September.

Sometimes, I get excited. And because of that, I give people ideas, ~~advice,~~ and answers without letting them come up with them themselves. Redaction. I don’t do that as much with giving advice. I used to do it earlier. Now, much less. I still do that with ideas and answers, so  
I don’t think I allow people to think. I jump in because I get excited. I don’t think that’s a particularly helpful thing. But what do you do when people specifically want that? I should give them ideas, ~~advice,~~ and answers, though, right? What’s the fine line there? 

With advice, it’s easier to gauge and ask people questions to figure out how they think, but how do I let people come to their own ideas and answers, what kind of questions do I ask to gauge how they think about ideas and answers? Well, advice is also a type of answer that people are looking for. I suppose I need to notice the questions that people ask more thoroughly. But sometimes, I hear people. But sometimes, I don’t hear well enough to sift through all the information. Find the signal from the noise. So, how do I practice that and gauge what someone is saying thoroughly? I think, in RWET, that’s one thing I loved about Allison: she listens so intently and transcribes so well what someone else is trying to say. I want to be able to transcribe better.

So, I noticed that I don't use the word 'realize' in the definition; that it is. I think I should use 'notice' instead.
