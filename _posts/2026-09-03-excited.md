6th September.

Sometimes, I get excited. And because of that, I give people ideas, ~~advice,~~ and answers without letting them come up with them themselves. Redaction. I don’t do that as much with giving advice. I used to do it earlier. Now, much less. I still do that with ideas and answers, so  
I don’t think I allow people to think. I jump in because I get excited. I don’t think that’s a particularly helpful thing. But what do you do when people specifically want that? I should give them ideas, ~~advice,~~ and answers, though, right? What’s the fine line there? 

With advice, it’s easier to gauge and ask people questions to figure out how they think, but how do I let people come to their own ideas and answers, what kind of questions do I ask to gauge how they think about ideas and answers? Well, advice is also a type of answer that people are looking for. I suppose I need to notice the questions that people ask more thoroughly. But sometimes, I hear people. But sometimes, I don’t hear well enough to sift through all the information. Find the signal from the noise. So, how do I practice that and gauge what someone is saying thoroughly? I think, in RWET, that’s one thing I loved about Allison: she listens so intently and transcribes so well what someone else is trying to say. I want to be able to transcribe better.

So, I noticed that I don't use the word 'realize' in the definition; that it is. I think I should use 'notice' instead.

[https://ell.stackexchange.com/questions/316442/what-is-the-difference-between-notice-and-realize](https://ell.stackexchange.com/questions/316442/what-is-the-difference-between-notice-and-realize)

These revelations are me noticing, not realisations, because I'm still figuring it out, but that's all of life. Maybe some revelations will be more certain over time.

I think there’s something really validating about what Allison said: stories being manipulative. I resonate so much with that. I’ve thought about it a lot. I don’t know how to write stories in that nuanced way. She didn't have an answer. I don’t have an answer. Or leave someone to interprete it. As Allison said, sometimes this manipulation is important to get a point across. Kezia and I were on a call three days before and were talking about this too. She, like Allison, talked about writing in observations, something which we also discussed in class. But even observations have this level of choice that you are making. It’s what you are perceiving. Well, doing this is making someone know your perspective, so it is egocentric in nature. So I question if my perspective is really important? Does it hold value? The way I see things? I worry about the words I use. The implications that it can have on people, even with a lot of care.  

Raviya and I were talking about the word ‘sensitive’ today. When we were talking about sensitivity, we had completely different definitions of it. She looks at it inwardly, not in the manner that I do. When she says someone is sensitive, it’s a negative connotation; they take things quite personally. When I say sensitive, I mean it in a manner that they try to empathise with other people’s emotions. Now, I try to be a sensitive person, actually. So I was telling Raviya about things I wrote about recently. Apparently they are quite hot takes. About my feelings about ‘I’m sorry’ not being as effective, and more. She said oh she would need to change the way she talks so I get what she means. That wasn't my intent. I don’t want her to change her natural mannerisms to match what I'm thinking, considering that I'm just figuring out what works for me and what doesn’t. These are what I'm noticing, not realising. I do process the world differently. Thus, I take a keen interest in people’s linguistics and mannerisms, but I don’t want people to change how they act around me as I give away my opinions; that’s exhausting for anyone. She says I don’t take things personally. I don’t, I think. I’m a sensitive person about how others feel, but that’s on me and not for others to manage. Something I need to learn to deal with. I do feel a lot more like myself now; I didn’t earlier.

The adjective [sensitive](https://www.merriam-webster.com/simple/sensitive) has so many definitions. 

I don’t understand social cues well, but that’s on me to figure out. So, I like when people are direct with me; even when it can be something that sounds mean or could potentially hurt me, it's better than the hiddenness of things. I like when people can be real with me. Most often, with time, I can empathise with where they are coming from. Amisha has told me I can come across as really annoying sometimes, but because they like me, they like how I am. So I told Raviya she doesn’t need to change the way she speaks around me to match what I feel; that would feel more hurtful because then she won’t be herself around me.
