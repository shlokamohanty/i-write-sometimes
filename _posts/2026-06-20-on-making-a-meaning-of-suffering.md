I got into this trap, that I have only two years to produce meaningful work. That all of my suffering is for a reason, in some ways, is the whole trope of ‘good damage’. But I’ve rather come to the acceptance that damage is just damage. There’s no deeper meaning to it. Making something out of it wouldn’t necessarily make me feel better. It sucked. I didn’t need to go through it. That’s it. My childhood was bad, uncomfortable, and painful. Trauma doesn’t amount to something. The brokenness is what makes people, people. It is what it is. I don’t need to audit my suffering.

What does that mean, and what is the challenge for my practice? I think, looking back at some of my work, like ‘Shadows by the blue light’, I tried to depict my personal story. While doing so, I struggled to come up with an ending to it, because there are still things from it which will remain with me, as they would for anyone from any kind of relationship. People just don’t come out of abusive relationships improved. It isn’t this big thing that everything gets better. I tried to make it into this thing of ‘good damage’. But, the world is cruel. My abuser got away with no consequences, and from what I heard, my abuser is getting married, and I’m still dealing with the cost of it through my anxiety. And I could sit in pity, wondering why I had to go through it, or perhaps look at it more objectively, that it’s just this thing that happened to me, and it’s over, and it’s this form of static, stable data, perhaps, now can be tapped into through analysis.

Now leaning more into autoethnography and away from confessional autobiography, the goal of autoethnography is never a personal story. It’s the qualitative analysis of your story. That my story isn’t only mine. It involves other people, places, and institutions, which largely make up the culture. That’s the intent with my thesis. I don’t want to make artwork with the intention of healing but rather with the intent of insight.
