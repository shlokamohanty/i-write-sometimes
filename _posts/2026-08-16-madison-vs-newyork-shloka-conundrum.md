I talked with Amisha, as usual, and now Megan is visiting me. Two people who have seen me grow and change over the years. 

First day as a human syndrome, I say. Talking to Amisha last week and today with Megan made me realise, I suppose, that I was going through something in Madison. The amount of apathy is hard to comprehend. 

Amisha told me that I talk about my feelings regularly with them so they get it. Megan, however, mentioned that I didn’t in Madison. She said, "Who is this person?" I chuckled. But Megan saw me regularly. Amisha only heard me talk about it. They weren’t with me when I was in Madison.

So, I was telling Megan all the different emotions that I felt, how I felt, which most people feel, and she was in shock that I feel like everyone else. In some ways, hearing that was extremely validating that I, in fact, have feelings. Strange, because people in New York see me as an extremely sentimental person. A strange thing: I wasn’t expressive, and now I am expressive to a fault. Megan and Amisha both said I was just really traumatized. Megan also said it makes sense why I’m so overwhelmed by feelings. 

---

Dystopian, I say. Megan was mentioning that she and some of our other friends were talking to each other and mentioned that if my ex and I ended, their faith in love would end simultaneously. So strange. Because I wasn’t even in that headspace. The disconnect felt humorous. They all just thought, that’s all I feel, and that’s it. But that’s scary. I realised in that moment that I do act more like myself only in a small group or in a one-on-one setting. And, most of undergrad, we used to hang out in a big group setting. And so that made me think about romantic relationships and the nature of that in general: what is really the meaning of it in my life. And from there I realised I have these predefined notions of what it should look like, from how I see it online, the patterns, what things people should do, shouldn’t do in a relationship, just things. And, in my head, I categorize acts and behaviors as mature and immature, and question whether I’m being mature, when in reality, it’s not as simple to box things in two categories. Outwardly, and maybe on paper, that relationship was supposed to be the relationship that people look up to, and people looked up to it. Yet, there was an absolute lack of love on my end, and things went wrong. I wasn’t at ease. I wasn’t feeling seen. It’s dizzying. Maybe I’ve just been following this fixed script in my head for so long. And now I’m René Descartes-ing it. Megan mentioned how my ex, in hypotheticals, frequently talked to her about teaching soccer to his kids and marriages. Which is funny. We never spoke about a future like that. We only spoke, for a week’s length, of futures. At that point, I couldn’t see myself get married at all, nor wanting kids. So, I wasn’t really reading into him and his wants. Maybe I do take words too literally for someone who writes in metaphors. But words themselves are perhaps poetry. What is poetry? And who speaks it? So, I think about my wanting to be chosen oftentimes. And I guess in doing so, I sometimes forget about choosing another, and seeing another truly for who they are? Megan mentioned that I’m someone who is always quite ambitious, and he wasn’t. She questioned, how did that not bother me? I was so focused on myself that I didn’t care. That’s all quite selfish, I suppose. 

---

I learnt the word ‘redamancy’ today. I liked it quite a bit. And we have these feelings and thoughts that show up, and you can feel them in your body. So, I spend a lot of time nowadays thinking about adjectives, like the ‘feeling’ words. So, I’m starting to dislike ‘happy’. And, when I say that, I imagine a world with just ‘happy’ people, and they sound like they live in oblivion, and that’s scary. I like ‘content’, ‘cheery’. I like the fleeting nature of emotions and their amalgamations. I’m developing qualms with the word ‘understanding’. I avoid the sentence, ‘I understand you’. I try saying, ‘I get you’ or ‘I appreciate you’.
