I had a dream that the time machine was hovering over something that looked like a zero. It sucked me in, and it was empty. Maybe because I had read yesterday that zeros can be reduced to empty sets. And without a clock, I woke up at seven in the morning. I went back to sleep because I knew I hadn't slept enough. The world looks strange when you wake up. Everything is blurry, the lights shifting shapes and spreading.

I have a routine, the same as it was in Minneapolis. The moment that I wake up. I make myself instant coffee. The hot water pouring into the cup feels like it's going through the zero, into the empty set. It's a Saturday morning, alone in my studio apartment in New York. The machine seems ready. I'll use it as I drink my coffee.

Waking up in Mumbai.

How different it felt waking up in different places. The machine takes me back. I remember when I was little, I would wake up to the sounds of the pressure cooker. It was loud. I can't get angry anymore. I just shut down if I'm too overwhelmed, but I used to scream at my mom, asking her to be more considerate. I was up late studying. Or maybe my meds made it a lot worse waking up. From the neighboring windows, the symphony went on.

When I was even younger, my mom would play 91.1 Radio, and I'd wake up listening to really old Hindi songs. It was calmer. I liked it.

Waking up in Minneapolis.

I couldn't bear to be just alone. I couldn't bear my thoughts, so I wanted Gir by my side every night. I hated anyone sleeping by my side then, though. I hated physical touch then as opposed to now. I'd move his arms from over me in the mornings. There were days when he left before me, so I'd wake up alone. I hoped he'd feel better that day. I'd send him more job postings. Minneapolis skies were filled with snow. The windows were covered with snow.

Gir is in DC now. I wake up by myself now. It's snowy in Minneapolis as it always is. The carpeted floors are making my room dusty. I stare at the traffic cone we stole together. I've had it with me in every house I've moved to for all three years in Minneapolis. I put dried flowers and stickers on it.

Some days, I'd wake up early, skid my way out, and study before anyone else was up. It was dark, but I liked the quiet, the few hours before sunrise. It felt like my own.

There were days too, I'd wake up and often hear the sink running or Maya brushing. Some days, I'd find Maya downstairs, wearing her green pants, a sweater of mine that I told her she could keep, running for class. She'd say good morning to me.

I remember waking up in the dorms in the first year when I'd sleep over at Raj's dorm. We are friends. He had an extra bed because Masey moved out. But I still slept on his Futon. His room was a mess. He had a kettle, and I made him make me tea at night. He loved glazed doughnuts from Kwik Trip. Reminded me to eat them in the mornings before my five-hour chemistry lab, through his sleep.

Used to sleep over at Akira's too on Wednesdays that year. Again, she had an extra bed too, because I had moved out. Again, left early in the morning to go for classes before she'd fully wake up. It was like that before I moved out.

But I remember waking up after the first time I experienced daylight saving time ending. The week before, I asked Reiyan if he wanted to try long distance. He said no. I always say things too late? Would he have said yes if I had asked him earlier? This was never meant to last. We started the relationship with a deadline. I couldn't wake up. I was heartbroken. So I'd wake up, and then close my eyes. I wanted the day to be over. Till I couldn't sleep anymore. So I'd sleep in till 4 pm.

I need to take time and build characters. This is a long-form writing project. I can't write directly from memory, then abstract minute parts of it. My true, most authentic, vulnerable storytelling comes out through letters. I wonder if I start writing in the letter form and then take it from there. Nah, that takes away from this.
