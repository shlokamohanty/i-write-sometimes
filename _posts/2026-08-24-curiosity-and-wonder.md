Sometimes, I’m in a hurry to write. Well, being in a hurry to write is alright because, in this case, it’s my personal blog, and if there are mistakes, that’s alright, I think, because I can fix them as I see. Sometimes, I’m in a hurry to push to Git often. But I should be slower and more careful, I think. 

It makes me question my organization system on this blog. In that case, shouldn’t almost all posts inherently become ongoing rather than past? I do sometimes revise my past reflections. Those are my qualms with my own organization systems. I don’t have it figured out really. Neither does anyone really, I think. I mean, that’s really the point of life, I suppose. I don’t quite know at this moment how to organize these posts in a manner that allows me to self-correct. And that maybe will require me to draw it out. What categories should I use, if not ‘ongoing’ and ‘past’ as labels? These labels almost signify time as a factor in my thinking, which is rather interesting. What factors do others think by? Do I just need to accept that organizational systems will keep changing?

---

So, I’ve realised that the manner in which I write - the more similarly I talk, the more it shapes my process of thinking, I think. So, maybe, if I write with slowness, I’ll think about things more slowly, rather than jumping to reach for answers and conclusions. And from there, it makes me think about the definition of ‘curiosity’ versus ‘wonder’.

[Curiosity](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/curiosity), as a noun, is an eager wish to know or learn about something

[Wonder](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/wonder), as a noun, is a feeling of great surprise and admiration caused by seeing or experiencing something that is strange and new

There’s something very keen about sitting with wonder rather than curiosity, I think. From what I’ve been reading lately, there is an astounding similarity between the feeling of awe and anxiety. These are mostly in opinion pieces. Why does curiosity feel rather anxious to me?

These late realizations. I wish I wrote more in undergrad. Rather than passing through information, I could have absorbed it and formulated it as my own means of formulating a meaning: knowledge. 

Keep writing, even embarrassingly so.

From [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_curiosity), intellectual curiosity is also known as epistemic curiosity. 

Strangely enough, I learnt earlier that epistemic uncertainty also arises from a lack of knowledge about the system or phenomenon of interest. Maybe curiosity is a means to fill that? That’s why, to me, it feels similar to anxiety.
