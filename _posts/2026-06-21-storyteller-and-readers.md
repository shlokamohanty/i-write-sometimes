I spent 2 hours yesterday with just a notebook and a pen. Not even any music. It was incredibly uncomfortable at first. But it helped me clear out a lot of the 'whys' I think about in conjunction with my life and thesis.

One thing that clicked for me, which started with RWET, was when the question of what understanding is was brought up. I was again thinking about "Nature's Folly": that art innately breaches loneliness, and the commonality of experiencing a similar feeling is understanding.

I had more questions about my thesis, the reasoning for doing it. Is it for people to understand me? Do I want to understand myself?

I went back to Schrödinger's cat thought experiment, and the act of opening the box. While spitballing about my thesis, I thought it was this form of vulnerability, but then yesterday, I thought maybe it's curiosity. Maybe vulnerability and curiosity go hand in hand. That you need to be vulnerable to be curious. That vulnerability is taking a step into the unknown knowing that there will be some discomfort. And curiosity is doing it anyway.

My ability to be hyper-observant can make people feel seen, but that is not the same as being understood. This ability helps me map people out. But, like chaos theory, people are unpredictable even if they all start at the same place. Identities shift. I know them, but I can't breach that isolation.

Which made me think of my relationships that made me feel understood, and the nature of conversations within those. Going back to how art breaches that isolation, in a lot of my relationships, I think even in conversations, I've felt understood by other people's stories. Good storytellers are good artists.

The other manner in which I think I've felt understood is in relationships, where people are extremely curious. They are good listeners, good prompters: good at prompting stories.

Realised that hyper-observance isn't curiosity. But it can be easily masked as curiosity. I realised I'm curious in an archaeological manner, so I ask people questions about their past. Made me think of how do I keep this curiosity in the present. Funny, at the age of 23, I've lost how to converse with people, maybe I didn't really have it before, maybe it was all just on the surface.

Made me think of the relationships where the people who asked me a lot of questions, and how they were curious about me. They were all readers and weren't intimidating. Made me conclude that curious people aren't intimidating, as they don't try to fit people into a model. But, I don't know where this feeling of intimidation is coming from, it's not from their judgement. In all my relationships, everyone has been judgmental, and none of them have been able to mask their judgment. Nor, would I want people to mask their judgements.

I end up rewatching a lot of the same movies and shows. I feel a sense of fear when starting a new movie, show, or book. Maybe I'm not brave enough to be vulnerable, and hence, I'm not curious enough.

Made me think that maybe I should read more fiction and watch new things. I like writing evidently, the storyteller in me, but I should read and watch more.
