I notice now that there is a big difference between seeing and perceiving, and I think I want to see more rather than perceive. My last conversation with Raviya about the word ‘sensitive’ made me realise how I wanted to see more than perceive. The perception comes from this lenses of analysing based on my experiences, I suppose, this is a little bit more in regards with people, but, before placing my analysis of people, I want to actually see them, as who they are, as I told Raviya, I didn’t want her to change how she talks, to match what I think, but, rather, have her talk and behave as she does, so I see her, and learn, rather than her react to my perception of people. That, in general, made me notice that I do want to see people more than perceive; that’s actually the best way to learn sometimes.

But, there is something larger here than just people. I like the nuance of things and complexities; thus, by seeing, I’m not immediately simplifying/labelling/boxing but rather taking in information as is. There is no categorisation. There is something here that I want to employ in how I want to develop my practice. A more thorough stance, from when I was ideating about my thesis.

Perception is not always reality. In a literal sense, though, everything is a perception.

So, it is rather impossible not to categorise; that’s how brains work, but in some manner, do that less? I suppose I’m already doing that by sitting with uncertainty more, accepting dynamism, and holding things loosely. I think I should let myself be proven wrong more often. Or allow more broader categories.
