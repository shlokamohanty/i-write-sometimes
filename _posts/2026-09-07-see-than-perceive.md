I notice now that there is a big difference between seeing and perceiving, and I think I want to see more rather than perceive. My last conversation with Raviya about the word ‘sensitive’ made me realise how I wanted to see more than perceive. The perception comes from this lens of analysing based on my experiences; I suppose this is a little bit more in regard to people, as opposed to things. Before placing my analysis of people, I want to actually see them as who they are. As I told Raviya, I didn’t want her to change how she talks to match what I think, but rather have her talk and behave as she does, so I see her and learn, rather than her reacting to my perception of people. That, in general, made me notice that I do want to see people more than perceive; that’s actually the best way to learn sometimes.

But, there is something larger here than just people. I like the nuance of things and complexities; thus, by seeing, I’m not immediately simplifying/labelling/boxing but rather taking in information as is. There is no categorisation. There is something here that I want to employ in how I want to develop my practice. A more thorough stance, from when I was ideating about my thesis.

But, at the same time, in a more literal sense, I'm actually entirely wrong here. You can't just see. Everything is a perception on some level. And perception is not always reality (seeing). Conundrum of wanting to see more, but, I actually cannot really see more; I'm always perceiving.

So, it is rather impossible not to categorise; that’s how brains work, but in some manner, do that less? I suppose I’m already doing that by sitting with uncertainty more, accepting dynamism, and holding things loosely. I think I should let myself be proven wrong more often. Or allow broader categories.

[https://philosophy.stackexchange.com/questions/136126/what-is-the-difference-between-observation-and-perception](https://philosophy.stackexchange.com/questions/136126/what-is-the-difference-between-observation-and-perception)

What I gather is that maybe the word I'm looking for is observation as opposed to seeing. Perception is more scientific; by that, I mean that there is a deduction involved. Actually, observation is also deduction. I suppose what I mean is to observe, but not try to find a meaning behind the observation. The meaning would make it a perception.
