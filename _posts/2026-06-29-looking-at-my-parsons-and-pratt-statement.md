I have always had a multitude of interests. Interactive arts provide a way to blend these interests by exploring critical issues within a growing digital culture. It is a gateway where art intersects with science, allowing me to unify my diverse passions instead of choosing just one.

"Shloka, you'll be a jack of all trades and a master of none," my family warned. They were concerned about my lack of a clear path since I wanted to pursue fashion design and environmental science. In 10th grade, I convinced my math teacher to help me explore the connections between hyperbolic paraboloids, art, and architecture, specifically focusing on Antoni Gaudí's Sagrada Família. However, I was advised that pursuing both paths could limit my time, so I chose the closest alternative: physics. This decision ultimately led me to abandon my dual dreams and focus solely on physics.

In university, I started as a physics major but quickly felt unfulfilled. I experimented with economics and data science, finding some solace in understanding human behavior while applying analytical skills through data science. Nevertheless, it wasn't enough, and I returned to filmmaking as a hobby. It became clear that filmmaking was essential for uniting my diverse interests. During this time, I created a short film titled "Puppet Hands," which tells a story through hands. I aimed to show how hands can convey much more than people often recognize, challenging my audience to empathize with shapes and objects rather than just humans. The film evokes feelings of being controlled by a puppeteer during periods of depression or depersonalization, illustrating how breaking out of that cycle can be incredibly relieving. At the time, I was navigating a similar period in my life, operating on autopilot and adhering to others' expectations. Eventually, I took control of my life and embraced all my interests.

I discovered the Interactive Arts and Digital Arts MFA program at Pratt, which aligns perfectly with my interests. It offers insight into technology's impact on our lives and involves research, prototyping, and writing. 

As a queer person from India, where discussions about gender and sexual identity are often limited, I aim to use my art and technology to bring these issues to light. I plan to work with Professor Uzma Z. Rizvi and collaborate with the Laboratory for Integrated Archaeological Visualization and M-Lab. My focus will be on exploring the socio-political dynamics of Mohenjo-Daro. My research will interpret clues related to gender and sexual identity found in art and artifacts, utilizing animation and visual art to foster empathy among audiences.

Additionally, through initiatives like STEAMplant, I envision projects that merge creative practices with social impact—such as using soft robotics for medical deliveries and designing sustainable water purification systems. By integrating STEM with art, I hope to spark dialogue on technology, accessibility, and ethics.
