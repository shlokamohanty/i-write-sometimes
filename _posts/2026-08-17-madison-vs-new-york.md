The New York vs the Madison Shloka conundrum, I say. The confrontation of the twos I met with when Megan visited me. 

She reminded me of how I used to be in Madison. Now, I don’t have to run from my house at 4:50 pm to drop off my physics homework at the mailbox at Chamberlin Hall by 5 pm. I don’t have to give an exam that starts from Fridays and goes on to Mondays.

Some days, I miss that structure that was given to me. I could be on autopilot. But then, I think, for the first time, I'm actually absorbed. When I write now, I think, I feel this warmth in my chest, something flowing from within. 

And so, I think I feel different.

I spend more time in my day thinking about words and their meanings. Retracing the meaning of each word and its formation at the tips of my tongue. 

Maybe because, for the longest time, I was at a loss of words, and I’m finally finding my way back. I think I used to feel unsettled when anyone wanted to get to know me. I didn’t like being perceived. Maybe because I had gone so long without being seen, and the comfort that I found in that.

I ask fewer questions now, she said, than I did before. She said I asked 'what' questions before. Thus, it seemed like I was listening more, because of so. I was at a loss for words, then. She doesn't know I couldn’t hear words then. I couldn’t tell her that sometimes in Madison, I was so dissociated that I wasn’t listening as thoroughly, so I picked up the key words to follow along - a manner to subtly nudge her to repeat so I could memorise her words. I listen more intently now, I think. I don’t rush to break people’s flow as they are talking. I don’t hand out solutions and opinions immediately to relieve my anxiety for them. I let them figure things out. She’s right, though, so I ask less, so I should nudge people to answer more, 'how's, and, 'why's, rather than 'what's, and try to appreciate how another thinks and thus feels, or feels and thus thinks, or just thinks or just feels. She remembers a version of me from three years ago.

So, as I’ve told Amisha, I tell Megan that I have a hard time telling cues. Half the time, when I'm in my head, I try guessing what someone else is feeling. It's wrong most of the time. And I tell her she’s good at telling how people feel, but she said that she has a hard time reading me. I try to pick up from both of them, and, how they read people. 

She said it’s hard reading me. She’s really observant, but, most often she gets wrong what I’m feeling. My facial expressions sometimes do not match what I’m feeling. I’d be having a super fun time, and a grouchy face. She thought I wanted to throw up after eating an ice cream, but I was actually really enjoying the ice cream. Sometimes, Raviya has moments like that too. She thinks I’m not enjoying, but in fact I'm enjoying a lot. Maybe, after a while, I actually had more words for Megan than I did ever before. I think I should be more direct with people and tell them what I think, what I'm feeling, even if it sounds rather uncanny.

She said that I used to remember more. She said I could remember conversations with the exact date and moment, time sometimes even, and what was said verbatim, but now it takes me a second, and then I go, 'Oh, you’ve already told me this', maybe because I’m still playing catch-up with her life, learning the names, and constructing her tree. Information overload. I'm making trees in my head from learning about her, and everyone in New York. It was easier when we talked every day, and in Madison, I only had to memorise very few people's lives. Exact dates and moments and times seems like an exaggeration. I don’t think I have a memory that apt. Things from college aren’t as clear in my head anymore. It’s cloudy. I was going through something. The fog will lift soon. So, I’d better carry the parts of Madison Shloka too. It became clearer with Megan here. I think, though, with people in New York, I can remember a lot of conversations to quite a clear degree.

But now Megan knows, after years, what I think I'm feeling like. She said, in Madison, I wouldn’t have shared as much. I was telling her a story that left me unsettled during my time at undergrad. She said even if she would have shared something similar, I wouldn’t have shared this with her then, and I said yes. We’ve always had drifting levels of closeness, but I do feel closer now.

So she said, now I know, that being vulnerable and open about my feelings is important. Instead of being completely apathetic and then suddenly feeling to a fault, I can just try and sit with emotions, with more regularity.

So I think about words. And after a while I'm actually hearing, listening, reading words without them just floating around in the sky. I’m finding my way back to words. The New York vs the Madison Shloka conundrum, I say. It’s more of a Venn diagram now.
