The photo is unrelated, or perhaps shows the whimsy, or maybe it’s not as deep, and it’s just a selfie. But I love when people describe me as silly and sweet. Trying to be my silliest and sweetest version of myself.

I’m a sweet person. I make silly decisions, and I sometimes (often) wonder why. I want to be the sun in people’s lives, light up their days, and be as warm as I can be.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-relationship2.jpg)

I had never really taken a photo of myself crying, but I thought why not, and it was definitely coming to an acceptance and allowing myself to feel sad more freely.

But sometimes I’m the moon, and sadness can really suck me in. Something about being sad is more profound than I can ever imagine, so I’m trying to embrace that. It is truly a feeling that makes you reflect on life through a lens that looks at the world with so much care, and perhaps that’s why I want to be the sun. Perhaps, I’m both.

At my lowest, growing up, I’d listen to song, 'I Always Wanna Die (Sometimes)', on repeat. I listen to songs very deeply, more than I thought. So it’s hard to revisit certain songs, and this was probably one of those. I love the lyrics, “If you can’t survive, just try.” I realise that growing up rather depressed, it does show up in ways, even as you grow older, even though you are in a better space. I used detachment as a way to escape that, but I think I’m growing to accept the sadness within me, as a manner of becoming a better person too, someone that can be sweet and silly, and even perhaps happy.

Lately, I’ve been thinking a lot about relationships in my life, with people around me. It dawned on me the day before: I don’t really ask people the right kind of questions. I spend most of my days on the floor at ITP. I don’t really ask people questions about what’s going on with them outside of the floor. I actually don’t make much of an effort to get to know them beyond their work. I realised I don’t ask people many questions about how they are doing. I have been thinking of two conversations that I had with people on the floor, well one was a 40 min conversation yesterday, I caught myself asking the right kind of questions, and I had barely spoken to this person before in my life, but, as I was asking questions, I learnt a lot about him, and his story, and then I realised, I should make more of an effort to get to know people in that manner, at least within my floor. Then, the day before, I was talking to another person, and I started asking more questions about him and his partner, and while doing so, I started realising more things that are important to him, and I realised that because I asked the right kind of questions, for the first time, I was actually able to learn so much more. Now, what the right kind of questions are, I don’t know. I’m figuring out, and I’m learning, sometimes, it’s easier to come up with them, sometimes, it takes longer, but I’m realizing I should make more of an effort to get to know people more closely, at least on the floor. Be more caring, perhaps.

The thing is, these conversations happen really naturally at home, which is something I have to think about, I suppose. When you’re living with someone, it becomes much more natural for me to get to know them more intently. I was talking to my college roommate about this, and she told me that we had known each other for a whole year, built a foundation, and then in the house, having these conversations just became something that naturally occurred. Even now, when I told my roommates that I don’t ask the right questions to the people in my cohort, they couldn’t believe that’s true. Because, I ask them many questions about themselves, trying to get to know them better.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-relationship3.jpg)

I love this photo of my brother. It’s in my room with the sun falling on his face. He was the cutest baby ever.

One of the relationships in my life that constantly evolves and changes is with my brother. I have to be very careful about which aspects of my life I share with him, and he feels that way, too, sometimes, but it keeps changing. Now we talk and share more with each other. A ten-year age gap is always weird. The older he gets, the closer that we both feel to each other because I can share a lot more about my life, and he feels that way too. With that age gap, you always have to think about what you say. It’s been really nice talking to him lately, though, really fun, in fact, even though we are continents apart.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-relationship4.jpg)

Now, romantic relationships. Or rather, the kind that I fail at so badly, I think, but I’ve come to realise, not particularly, I’ve had some successful romantic relationships, even though they ended, the good ones, all of them remain so etched in my heart. I was recently talking to someone, and I told them, “Ya, if I have a crush on someone, I’d tell them,” but the truth is I realised I would have done that four years ago. I pride myself on having no ego and being unafraid of rejection. Well, I’m not afraid of rejection, per se, but because most romantic relationships have ended badly, I don’t say anything to preserve the relationship. I’ve done this twice in my life. I liked this guy a lot, freshman year of undergrad, and I realised it then too, but I never told him, never told anyone, until later. He still doesn’t know I liked him, that’s funny, I think. We are still friends. But I thought, telling him I liked him then would ruin our friendship, so instead, I started dating my ex. Ya, that decision-making didn’t make any sense. I thought I’d like my ex over time, but I didn’t, but I stayed with him for three years—ahh. I dated another guy in school for a month. I call him the most embarrassing ex, for many reasons. He ruined my sleep schedule for a month and also ended up cheating on me. Well, it’s okay, I didn’t really like him, I liked the guy I dated after him, even while dating him. He asked me out, around the same time, this guy did, but I didn’t end up dating him then, even though I liked him, because he was friends with my other ex with whom, I was in an abusive relationship with, which I think is a fair reason even now, but I could have told him, I liked him, and maybe waited to see his response, then, instead of dating another guy. Anyways, perhaps, I need to live up to my words and actually be less afraid of romantic feelings as such. There’s no point as such in burying those feelings without even trying.

Well, over the winter break, and even recently, I’ve been thinking of those successful romantic relationships and writing about them.

I broke four years of no contact, one day, and sent someone a story I wrote about them.

Summary of how that conversation went over text and my thoughts:

> His first reaction was, “wow”. I always wondered what it would be like if we had a conversation years later, and I had a lingering scenario in my head of the person saying, “How are you doing?” So he did ask me that, and I told him, I’m doing good. I told him I’m doing my master’s at NYU. He told me, “oh wow, vibes congrats”. I was pretending not to know what he’s been up to, but he caught me lying. It was funny, we texted for probably five minutes. Then I asked him more questions, and he left me on read.

I wrote about this girl, with whom I had a confusing relationship.

Summary of how that conversation went over text and my thoughts:

> She told me she was in love with me but couldn’t say it. She felt kind of angered reading the text, thinking of what could have been because there was so much unsaid between us. She told me that she told my ex all these feelings she had for me, and I didn’t know that. My ex then continued pursuing me, and that upset me. She was upset that I had distanced myself from her.

Having those conversations at this point in my life felt cathartic, now that I’m over those relationships. I spoke with my college roommate, who pointed out that we still have a good relationship because we know how to handle conflicts and differences of opinion. In my past romantic relationships, differences always caught up with us, but while those relationships lasted, they were good. That’s what makes them successful in my eyes and changes how I look at people. Writing these stories and having these conversations brought me warmth: it showed me that relationships can change and evolve. Even romantic relationships that didn’t last can be remembered fondly, without holding grudges or feeling hurt. At least the good ones.

I do make stupid decisions, but I learn more each time, and I’m a sweet and silly person who keeps growing and wants to keep growing, and I like that, rather.
