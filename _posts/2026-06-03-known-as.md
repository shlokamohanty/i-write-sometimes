Would I want to be known as an inventor and an artist? Maybe an inventor over an artist, though. Perhaps, neither in my lifetime.

Why an inventor over an artist? The term inventor is intertwined with STEM, especially when you look at history and who is called an inventor. But this isn’t stemming from wanting to be remembered. I don’t particularly care to be remembered. Something that I _create_ is new, makes a small difference in someone’s life, and that is all that matters to me. Or maybe it’s the fact that I grew up watching ‘Phineas and Ferb’. It’s more so setting myself a challenge of a lifetime: do I have the capabilities to push the boundaries to _create_ something new? Turning ideas that are extremely abstract into something tangible? Because inventions are tangible, I think. I don’t know if that is possible in my lifetime, but maybe something I can aspire to do.

I wonder if inventions can evoke emotions the way art does. Maybe it’s in conjunction. But why an inventor over an artist? Because being an artist comes with responsibility. Something I find a tad bit manipulative is that artists know what kind of emotion their artwork would evoke — something I’ve figured out from my own work. In my work and in my writings, there’s a lot of sentimentality. When work is sentimental, it becomes easy to tell how someone will react. I want to step away from that, speak with more rawness and honesty, and be unaware of the reaction it evokes. I’ve found myself making things as a means of self-expression, not understanding the emotions I’m pouring in while making, but eventually, after it’s done, I process what I was really feeling. When I _make_ things for others, it feels more meaningful. Making art about myself and my feelings feels narcissistic. But I’ve been thinking about auto-ethnography — how it combines the self and larger culture to turn personal stories into analytical studies, treating oneself as an experiment, looking at experience objectively as data.

If I _make_ art that way, it may _create_ something, something I hope, or maybe even more than an invention — it sparks curiosity in others, and perhaps I learn a thing or two from it. I don’t want all my artwork to be that, but maybe some.

An internal dilemma that I think about often is which labels and tags I may or may not want to wear. Maybe **freak** is the better word.
