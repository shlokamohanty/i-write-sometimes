An intimate question, certainly. I’ve always liked the idea of what a girl does in her room, because for some reason, mainstream media shows mostly men and their experiences in a room, and even the depictions of women and non-binary people in their rooms are shown in the most stereotypical manner, which is not really relatable. Growing up mostly alone, the room became an interesting space for me. The four walls have seen a lot and will continue to do so.

![me](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room1.jpg)

So, for one of my classes, ‘Cabinets of Wonder’, we are tasked with making a diorama with a narrative, and Tess and I came up with the idea of using the walls of rooms as something that changes, while the furniture stays the same, which is something we’ve both experienced moving often, at least during undergrad. Tess likes fabricating, and I like illustrating, so we divided the work accordingly. So I started going over my photos from the different rooms I’ve lived in and illustrating the walls.

![my room when I was a kid](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room2.jpg)

This wasn’t the first place that I’ve lived growing up. We moved around a bunch before, so I don’t remember those, but this house was one I do remember. Well, I don’t remember all that much. One vivid memory, though, of this nightmare that I thought was a very real experience was of a huge mosquito chasing me to eat me, and somehow getting through the mosquito net. I didn’t let anyone in the house sleep that night. And then for a whole month, I was scared to play outside because I thought the mosquitoes would eat me.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room3.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room4.jpg)

This was my favorite room, even to date. I loved the color, and I loved Winnie the Pooh, still do. I have many memories from this room and this house. I remember eating aloo _paratha_ and yogurt, and sometimes, with mango pickle, in my room. Drinking a lot of _kesar_-flavored _Amul Kool_. My best friend used to draw on the walls in my room. Doing handstands and full splits on my bed. I almost broke my arm doing a handstand, then I was too scared to do handstands. I remember being sad that I didn’t have a common name, so there were no songs with my name. Start of my mom’s pregnancy.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room5.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room6.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room7.jpg)

This room is haunting, yet I’m grateful for it. I hated the room, though. I hated pink. The colour then seemed so gendered. Even then, I questioned my gender. For some reason, even to this date, I feel more comfortable being called a girl than a woman. Well, I guess, lately I’ve been feeling more girly, perhaps. The other parts of the room were green. The combination then was just really ugly to me. So I hated the wall colours. We moved within the same society. It wasn’t much of a change, but this was the house I grew up in. My parents don’t live there anymore. I loved city lights. As did everyone on Tumblr that time. But it was interesting playing around with your reflection, and I did that a lot.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room7.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room8.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room9.jpg)

I was really obsessed with shadows and silhouettes. I used to come back from school and go directly into my room. Often, I’d end up napping for a bit, but during the winter, the golden hours were really pretty, and a lot of sun would come in. I miss the sun coming in from my window. That was something I really liked watching. I was really into taking photos of my shadows during this time, even while walking outside, and otherwise, but the sun coming into my room always made me feel lighter. I used to have a nursery rhyme kind of saying in my head that I’d repeat to myself even on bad days, “the sun is shining very brightly, my day is going very rightly”. I don’t know to this day if I picked it up from someone or somewhere, but during that time, most days were miserable, so anything small, like the sunlight, would keep me going. Anyways, that’s why I’m going to use shadows to play with for the short film that I’m making for one of my classes.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room10.jpg)

I forgot at some point that I didn’t really have a ceiling because the water pipe from the house upstairs was leaking for a while, and the ceiling walls needed to be removed because they were swelling up and could have fallen on me. Then the whole room was re-coloured.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room11.jpg)

For a whole month, my cupboards looked like this. I thought putting it on my cupboard would help me remember concepts. Maybe I’d wake up and get to it. This was the first time in my life that I was forced to adhere to a strict schedule, so I put everything on my calendar. I wasn’t used to sticking with a tightly planned routine. Now, maybe I could manage, but back then it felt exhausting.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room12.jpg)

I’ve always liked the arts and sciences about equally, and it’s still the same. It’s weird, when I’m too focused on one, I miss the other. I still don’t know how to integrate both to the level of intensity that I want, or whether I ever will. But, if I’m not, I know I need to do both, in some capacity or the other, in my life all throughout, that is my thing, and that will keep me going, truly.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room13.jpg)

I always made a mess while studying. I did that during undergrad, too, and I still do. Perhaps I desperately needed a folder then, and I still do. My physics teacher then told me that because I’m messy, I’m really creative, which is funny. I don’t really see messiness as a sign of creativity, but it was nice to hear someone say I’m creative. My parents thought otherwise then.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room14.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room15.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room16.jpg)

Now, I found these photos and a video that made me think of the ITP shop, but it was my room growing up. In Thane, there was one area where you’d get all the electronics, micro-controllers, and everything you’d want. It was near a theatre called _Prabhat Talkies_. My first time being introduced to an Arduino was here. I remember Amisha and me walking down random _gallies_ to find specific diodes. In 8th grade, we tried making a pulse meter. I remember soldering and everything. Did the project work? No. I saw the circuit diagram recently, and I was sad thinking that my 8th-grade self was perhaps smarter. Amisha reminded me that one of our class teachers re-soldered everything, and my friend’s brother helped us with the code, and that’s when I was like, okay, that makes more sense. In 11th grade, again, I tried to make an electromagnetic levitation device at home for one of my internal assessments during COVID. Of course, that didn’t work either, but I loved working on these. I still scope too big. I don’t know how my parents let me solder at home without the proper ventilation. My poor floor.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room17.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room18.jpg)

Now in Wisconsin, I lived in 8 places. I moved into Waters Residence Hall in my second semester of undergrad. I loved that room and Waters. There were two windows in my room, and the view outside was that of Observatory Hill, and I loved that. Living in Waters, you could really see the seasons change, and it was really beautiful to see that happen, the leaves coming in, and how the sun starts setting later. Waters has also seen me at my lowest and picking myself up during that semester.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room19.jpg)

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room20.jpg)

So, I did move into another apartment during the summer before moving into this one. The living room in that apartment was nonexistent. Well, before this, I wasn’t really allowed to put up much on my wall, so the moment I had the chance to put up a lot on my wall, I did. I put a little too much, actually. But it’s really fun to look back at now. My ex and I had started dating over the summer, right before I moved in. So, in the first six months, we’d spend a lot of time together in this room studying. It was rather nice, then.

![](/i-write-sometimes/assets/images/image-room21.jpg)

I loved the semester that I lived in this apartment. I worked, studied, did a credit overload, and made time for my friends. The balance was just perfect, I thought then. But I was doing too much, at once, didn’t have time to reflect on things, and barely slept. I’m learning the importance of taking time out. It was really fun, sweet, and academically a really good semester. Unfortunately, I had to move out of the apartment the next semester due to roommate conflicts.

Well, there were many rooms then. And there will be many more rooms and many more experiences. I haven’t reflected on a lot of the rooms. But thinking about this has been really fun, yet rather sad. Memories are always weird to look back at. They carry this pain, but in some ways shape who you are in the present. I think walls have carried those for me, and my room, in ways, becomes that marker of changes and the sameness of things.
