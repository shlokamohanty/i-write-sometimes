I was talking to Amisha about this extensively when I was in Allston this weekend. I asked them this question because they are a journalist, and they actually work with other people's stories, and I was telling them my initial thesis idea. I asked them, how do you tell other people's stories without taking away agency. They told me that artists aren't responsible enough to do that. They don't quite understand when filmmakers also talk about telling other people's stories. Journalists are taught the apparatus to carry this responsibility.

The form of abstraction and fiction in art becomes a means of license to make work. The manner to word it is that you can use other people's stories as information rather than say that you are 'telling other people's stories'.

The bigger questions in this would be whether the person consented to being one of the source materials, and do they benefit or get harmed by what I made, and does the abstraction protect them or just protect me from having to answer for how I used them. It's also using other people's stories. What are you trying to get from it?

Well, this is partly why I don't want to tell other people's stories anymore. But also, even when it comes to my memories, it involves other people. My life is entirely populated by others. I can't dig up a memory without the people attached to it. How do you abstract your memories enough to tell stories in a manner that doesn't take away agency?
